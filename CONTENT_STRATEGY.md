# Content Strategy — Repdoc

**Prepared:** 1 November 2026  
**Scope:** Organic search content for Repdoc’s public site  
**Primary market:** Bangladesh, Bangla-first  
**Primary competitor context:** `prottoyon.gov.bd`

---

## 1. Strategic position

Repdoc should not try to be a government portal. It should be the clearest independent toolkit for people who need to **prepare, format, print and verify their own documents**.

### 1.1 Positioning statement

> Repdoc helps you understand what a certificate usually requires, prepare a clean draft, and check a Repdoc-created document — while the official certificate is always issued by the relevant authority.

### 1.2 The content promise

Every public guide should be:

- **Specific** — one certificate or one task.
- **Practical** — documents, steps, links, caveats.
- **Honest** — no fake fees, timelines, approvals or government status.
- **Maintainable** — no facts that cannot be reviewed.
- **Bangla-first** — natural language, not translated keyword lists.

---

## 2. Audience

### 2.1 Primary audience

- Computer operators and small shop owners who prepare documents for local customers.
- Citizens who need a certificate and do not know the process.
- People helping family members with applications.
- Small business owners dealing with trade licences and attestations.

### 2.2 Secondary audience

- English/Banglish searchers looking for certificate formats.
- Users comparing “sonod” and “certificate” terminology.
- People who received a Repdoc document and want to verify it.

### 2.3 What they need

| Need | Content response |
|---|---|
| “What is this certificate?” | Definition and scope |
| “What do I need?” | Document checklist |
| “Where do I go?” | Authority explanation |
| “How long / how much?” | Variability and official confirmation |
| “Can I do it online?” | Local availability caveat |
| “Is this document valid?” | Verification guide |
| “Can Repdoc make it?” | Draft/print boundary |

## 5. Editorial rules

### 5.1 Accuracy

- Do not state a fee unless it is verified and maintained.
- Do not promise a processing time.
- Do not name a specific local office unless the page is authority-specific and verified.
- Do not imply government affiliation.
- Do not call a draft an “official certificate”.
- Do not claim QR verification of government documents.

### 5.2 Language

- Write in natural Bangla.
- Use common spelling variants once, not repeatedly.
- Keep sentences short.
- Use tables only for comparisons, not for keyword lists.
- Avoid transliteration clutter in the body; keep it in the “also searched as” line.

### 5.3 Headings

- One H1 per page.
- H2 for major sections.
- H3 for sub-questions.
- Do not use headings as keyword dumps.

### 5.4 Metadata

- Title: certificate/task + benefit + brand.
- Description: what the page covers + boundary.
- Canonical: self-referencing.
- OG image: relevant, not misleading.
- Schema: only what is visible.

## 6. Trust and E-E-A-T

Repdoc cannot claim government experience. It can demonstrate:

### Experience

- Show real product knowledge: what fields the generator asks for, what a print-ready draft looks like, what users commonly get wrong.
- Use screenshots or examples where helpful.
- Explain the difference between a draft and an issued certificate.

### Expertise

- Cite official sources where possible.
- Explain local variation instead of pretending one rule fits all.
- Use precise terminology and clarify synonyms.

### Authoritativeness

- Keep the about page, contact/social links and privacy policy consistent.
- Maintain the important-links directory.
- Avoid broken official links.
- Keep dates honest.

### Trust

- State the independent status on every guide.
- Do not fake reviews, ratings, user counts or government endorsement.
- Do not hide fees or login requirements.
- Make verification boundaries explicit.
- Protect privacy on verification result pages.

## 7. Internal linking

### 7.1 Hub-and-spoke

- `/sonod/` is the hub.
- Each guide is a spoke.
- Each guide links to 2–3 related guides.
- Each guide links to the certificate generator and verification.
- The certificate landing links to every guide.

### 7.2 Contextual links

- Link from a document checklist to the relevant tool.
- Link from a verification discussion to `/verify/`.
- Link from a fee/timeline caveat to the important-links directory.
- Do not add a giant “related links” block to every page.

### 7.3 Anchor text

Use descriptive anchors. Avoid exact-match repetition and “click here”.

## 8. Content gaps and next topics

### 8.1 Evidence-led additions

Add a guide only when at least one of these is true:

- Google Suggest shows a clear cluster.
- Search Console shows impressions with no matching page.
- Users repeatedly ask the same question.
- The topic supports an existing Repdoc tool.

### 8.2 Candidate topics

Consider only after measurement:

- নতুন ভোটার প্রত্যয়ন
- ভোটার তথ্য প্রত্যয়ন
- অনাপত্তি সনদ
- অবিবাহিত / বিবাহিত সনদ
- একই নাম প্রত্যয়ন
- আনুমানিক বয়স প্রত্যয়ন
- সনাতন প্রত্যয়ন
- প্রত্যয়নপত্র (অন্যান্য)
- Repdoc draft status (only if a real feature exists)

### 8.3 Topics to avoid

- Official application status.
- Government fee tables without a maintenance owner.
- Dual citizenship (unless researched and verified).
- Living person’s heir certificate (legally sensitive).
- District-by-district doorway pages.
- “Government certificate online” claims.

## 9. Update cadence

### Monthly

- Check Search Console queries and indexing.
- Fix broken internal links.
- Review any guide with a sudden impression drop.
- Check the sitemap count and robots.txt.

### Quarterly

- Review all 12 guides for accuracy.
- Update dates only where content changed.
- Re-check the keyword CSV.
- Review competitor structure, not content.
- Audit verification privacy.

### Annually

- Full technical SEO audit.
- Full content inventory.
- Remove or consolidate weak pages.
- Review domain/canonical strategy.

---

## 10. Distribution

Content does not rank by existing. Support it with:

- Navigation links.
- Footer links.
- Links from the certificate generator page.
- Links from relevant template pages.
- Social posts that answer one question at a time.
- YouTube/Telegram/Facebook posts linking to the guide, not just the homepage.
- Outreach to computer-operator communities, without spam.

Do not buy links or mass-post the same anchor text.

---

## 11. Measurement

### 11.1 Leading indicators

- Indexed pages.
- Sitemap discovery.
- Impressions per guide.
- Query coverage (queries the guide appears for).
- Internal clicks from hub to guides.

### 11.2 Outcome indicators

- Clicks to the generator.
- Verification page usage.
- Account registrations, if trackable.
- Returning users.
- Branded searches for Repdoc.

### 11.3 What not to measure

- Raw word count.
- Number of pages published.
- Keyword density.
- Rankings without impressions/click context.

## 12. Governance

| Decision | Owner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| New guide | Content + product | Must answer a real query and fit a real tool |
| Fee/timeline claim | Content lead | Must be verified and dated, or omitted |
| New location page | SEO + product | Must pass the location quality checklist |
| Schema change | Developer | Must match visible content |
| Verification change | Developer + privacy | Lookup results must stay noindex |
| Canonical change | Developer | Requires a migration plan |

---

## 13. Bottom line

Repdoc’s content strategy is not “publish more certificates”. It is “be the most trustworthy independent explanation of the certificate preparation process, and connect that explanation to a real drafting tool”.

The 12 guides establish the pattern. The next phase should be measured, reviewed, and deliberately narrow.
